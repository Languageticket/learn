What is the CEFR Framework?

The Common European Framework of Reference for Languages (CEFR) was established by the Council of Europe in 2001 (and expanded in the 2020 Companion Volume) to provide a transparent, coherent, and comprehensive basis for language syllabus elaboration, curriculum guidelines, examinations, and textbooks across the globe.

Unlike subjective grading systems (such as "beginner", "intermediate", or "fluent"), the CEFR measures operational linguistic ability through descriptive "Can-Do" statements. These statements specify what a learner can produce, comprehend, mediate, and negotiate across all four communicative domains: Speaking, Listening, Reading, and Writing.

The 6 CEFR Proficiency Bands in Detail

The CEFR divides language competency into three primary tiers: Basic User (A1–A2), Independent User (B1–B2), and Proficient User (C1–C2). Below is the comprehensive metric breakdown for each milestone.

A1 • Breakthrough
Basic User

Can understand and use familiar everyday expressions and very basic phrases aimed at the satisfaction of needs of a concrete type. Can introduce themselves and others and can ask and answer questions about personal details such as where they live, people they know, and things they have.

Active Vocabulary500 – 700 Words
Speaking Speed40 – 60 Words / Min
Grammar CorePresent Simple, 'To Be', Pronouns
A2 • Waystage
Basic User

Can communicate in simple and routine tasks requiring a simple and direct exchange of information on familiar and routine matters. Can describe in simple terms aspects of their background, immediate environment, and matters in areas of immediate need.

Active Vocabulary1,000 – 1,500 Words
Speaking Speed60 – 80 Words / Min
Grammar CorePast Simple, Modals (Can/Could), Basic Comparatives
B1 • Threshold
Independent User

Can understand the main points of clear standard input on familiar matters regularly encountered in work, school, leisure, etc. Can deal with most situations likely to arise whilst travelling. Can produce simple connected text on topics that are familiar or of personal interest. Can describe experiences, events, dreams, and briefly give reasons and explanations.

Active Vocabulary2,000 – 3,000 Words
Speaking Speed80 – 100 Words / Min
Grammar CorePresent Perfect, Conditionals 1 & 2, Passive Voice
B2 • Vantage
Independent User • Workplace Ready

Can understand the main ideas of complex text on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in their field of specialization. Can interact with a degree of fluency and spontaneity that makes regular interaction with native speakers quite possible without strain for either party. Can produce clear, detailed text on a wide range of subjects.

Active Vocabulary4,000 – 5,500 Words
Speaking Speed110 – 130 Words / Min
Grammar CorePast Perfect, Mixed Conditionals, Advanced Phrasal Verbs
C1 • Effective Operational
Proficient User

Can understand a wide range of demanding, longer texts, and recognize implicit meaning. Can express ideas fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions. Can use language flexibly and effectively for social, academic, and professional purposes. Can produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects.

Active Vocabulary7,000 – 9,000 Words
Speaking Speed130 – 150 Words / Min
Grammar CoreInversion, Cleft Sentences, Subjunctive, Discourse Markers
C2 • Mastery
Proficient User • Native-Equivalent

Can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read. Can summarize information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation. Can express themselves spontaneously, very fluently, and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in the most complex situations.

Active Vocabulary14,000+ Words
Speaking Speed140 – 170 Words / Min
Grammar CoreFull Idiomatic Mastery, Nuanced Pragmatic Register
